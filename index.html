Cyber Security • Internship Task 3

Cyber Threat Identification

Recognize common cyber threats, understand their potential impact, and learn practical ways to reduce risk.

Why awareness matters

Many security incidents begin with a deceptive message, unsafe download, manipulated request, or exposed password. Recognizing warning signs and reporting concerns early can reduce harm.

Remember: Never share passwords or one-time verification codes. When a request feels urgent or unusual, verify it using a separate trusted channel.

Threat classification

Threat 01

Phishing

What it is: Fake messages or websites impersonating trusted sources to steal information or prompt unsafe actions.

Targets: Email users, students, employees, customers.

Impact: Account compromise, financial loss, data exposure.

Prevention: Check sender addresses and domains, avoid unexpected links, navigate directly to known sites, report suspicious messages, and enable MFA.

Threat 02

Malware

What it is: Malicious software such as spyware, trojans, or worms.

Targets: Phones, computers, servers, organizations.

Impact: Data theft, device disruption, unauthorized access.

Prevention: Install updates, use reputable security tools, download from trusted sources, limit privileges, and maintain backups.

Threat 03

Ransomware

What it is: Malware that blocks access to files or systems, often by encrypting data, and demands payment.

Targets: Individuals, schools, hospitals, businesses.

Impact: Downtime, data loss, recovery costs, possible data exposure.

Prevention: Keep offline or immutable backups, patch systems, restrict access, train users, and test recovery plans.

Threat 04

Social engineering

What it is: Manipulating people into revealing information or performing unsafe actions.

Targets: Staff, help desks, students, administrators.

Impact: Fraud, unauthorized access, information disclosure.

Prevention: Verify unusual requests through a second channel, follow approval procedures, and keep sensitive details private.

Threat 05

Credential theft

What it is: Stealing passwords, session tokens, or other login information.

Targets: Email, social media, financial, and workplace accounts.

Impact: Account takeover, identity misuse, further breaches.

Prevention: Use unique passwords and a password manager, enable MFA/passkeys, never share one-time codes, and review login alerts.

What to do if you suspect an incident

  • Stop interacting with suspicious messages, links, attachments, or pop-ups.
  • Do not provide passwords or verification codes.
  • Report the concern to the relevant IT/security contact or platform.
  • If an account may be compromised, use a trusted device to change the password and revoke active sessions.
  • If malware is suspected, contact IT support; avoid risky self-repair and preserve relevant evidence.
  • Use known-good backups only after the affected system has been assessed.

Interview questions

What is phishing?

Phishing is an attack where someone impersonates a trusted person or organization to trick a target into clicking a malicious link, opening an unsafe attachment, or revealing sensitive information.

What is ransomware?

Ransomware is malicious software that blocks access to files or systems, commonly by encrypting data, and demands payment. Payment does not guarantee recovery.

What is social engineering?

Social engineering uses psychological manipulation to persuade people to disclose information, grant access, or take actions that weaken security.
